🐒 Chimp Test

Based on research at Kyoto University where chimpanzees outperformed humans in rapid spatial memory tasks, this test challenges you to memorize the positions of numbered tiles and click them in ascending order after they are hidden.

🐒

Chimp Test

Are you smarter than a chimpanzee? Memorize number locations before they turn into blank tiles.

How to Use Chimp Test

  1. Click "Start" to begin the test.
  2. Numbers will briefly appear on a grid of tiles.
  3. After the numbers disappear, click the tiles in ascending numerical order (1, 2, 3...).
  4. If correct, the next round adds one more number to memorize.
  5. The test ends when you click the wrong tile.

What This Test Measures

This test tracks your rapid spatial working memory: the ability to quickly memorize the positions of multiple items in a spatial layout and recall their order. Chimpanzees are remarkably good at this specific type of memory task.

Why Use This Test

The Chimp Test is a fascinating way to benchmark your spatial memory against a well-known cognitive research standard. It challenges a specific type of rapid visual encoding that differs from verbal or sequential memory.

How to Interpret Your Results

Reaching level 7–8 matches the average human performance. Levels 9+ demonstrate strong spatial memory. In the original research, trained chimpanzees consistently reached levels that most human participants could not match.

Tips for Accurate Results

  • Focus on the spatial pattern rather than trying to read each number individually.
  • Try to take a mental "snapshot" of the entire grid during the display phase.
  • Start by locating the lowest numbers first, since you need to click in ascending order.
  • Consistent practice can improve your spatial encoding speed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is it called the Chimp Test?

Based on Kyoto University research where chimpanzees outperformed humans in rapid working memory tests.

Can humans actually beat chimpanzees at this test?

Some practiced humans can reach high levels, but the original research showed that trained chimpanzees consistently outperformed most human participants at rapid spatial number recall.

What cognitive skills does the Chimp Test target?

It specifically targets rapid spatial working memory: the ability to quickly encode and recall the positions of items in a visual layout, which is a distinct skill from verbal or sequential memory.
