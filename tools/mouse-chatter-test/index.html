鼠标双击误触测试

Test your double-clicking speed and detect faulty mouse switch hardware chatter with millisecond precision.

How to Use 鼠标双击误触测试

  1. Click inside the test area.
  2. The tool records the time interval between consecutive clicks in milliseconds.
  3. Intentionally double-click to see your natural double-click speed.
  4. To test for chatter, click once firmly and check if a second click registers unintentionally.
  5. Intervals under 40–50ms on a single click likely indicate switch chatter.

What This Test Measures

We track the exact time between your clicks. This helps catch intentional double-clicks and accidental chatter: false double-clicks caused by degraded micro-switches.

Why Use This Test

If your mouse is getting old, it might start double-clicking on its own. This tool helps you figure out if your mouse switch is actually failing.

How to Interpret Your Results

Intentional double-clicks typically register between 60–200ms apart. If you see intervals under 40ms when you are only clicking once, your mouse switch may be "chattering": registering false double-clicks due to mechanical wear. Consistent chatter under 20ms strongly suggests a failing micro-switch.

Tips for Accurate Results

  • Click firmly and deliberately with single clicks to test for chatter: do not try to double-click.
  • Repeat the single-click test 10+ times to check for intermittent chatter.
  • If chatter is detected, the issue is typically hardware-related and may require mouse replacement or switch repair.
  • Test both the left and right mouse buttons separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is mouse double click chatter?

Mouse chatter occurs when a degraded micro-switch registers two clicks involuntarily within less than 50 milliseconds.

How do I know if my mouse has chatter?

Click once firmly inside the test area. If the tool registers two clicks with a very short interval (under 40ms) when you only clicked once, your mouse likely has a chatter problem.

Can mouse chatter be fixed?

Some users fix chatter by adjusting the Windows double-click speed setting or using debounce software. However, chatter is usually a hardware issue caused by a worn micro-switch and may ultimately require mouse replacement or switch soldering repair.

Is chatter only a problem with old mice?

Chatter most commonly occurs with aged micro-switches, but it can also appear on new mice with defective switches. Even high-end gaming mice can develop chatter over time.
